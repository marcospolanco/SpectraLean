## Strategic Identification of Principal Investigator Partners for Spectral and Nonequilibrium Network Research

The identification of elite academic partners capable of leading multi-disciplinary research programs necessitates a rigorous assessment of technical intersectionality, institutional gravity, and collaborative readiness. In the pursuit of developing programs adjacent to the concept of the spectral self—specifically those involving self-organizing, self-referential, or autonomous dynamical structures emergent from network topologies—a potential Principal Investigator (PI) must demonstrate mastery over five core technical pillars. These pillars encompass spectral methods on graphs, dynamical systems, nonequilibrium thermodynamics, functional analysis, and mathematical physics. The following report provides a curated hierarchy of PI-caliber researchers who possess the requisite rigor and leadership signals to serve as primary investigators on large-scale grant proposals, where specialized system design and implementation function as the critical subcontracting components.

## **Technical Pillars and Adjacency Criteria**

The success of a research program in this domain is predicated on a PI's ability to bridge abstract mathematical theory with the physical realities of complex networks. The technical coverage required for a "Prime" PI involves a sophisticated understanding of the graph Laplacian as a generator of diffusion and a measure of connectivity, coupled with the stability analysis of nonlinear systems and the thermodynamic constraints of open, dissipative structures.1

### **Spectral Graph Theory and Operator Analysis**

The foundational layer of the search focuses on researchers who treat networks as discretized manifolds. Expertise in graph Laplacians, spectral clustering, and eigenvalue bounds is non-negotiable.4 Candidates are evaluated on their ability to utilize normalized and unnormalized Laplacians to characterize community detection and diffusion processes.1 Higher-order spectral methods, including the study of hypergraphs and simplicial complexes, represent a significant advantage, as they allow for the modeling of many-body interactions beyond pairwise links.5

### **Dynamical Systems and Network Stability**

The evolution of states over network topologies is a core requirement. PIs in this archetype often specialize in synchronization phenomena, such as the Kuramoto model, where they utilize spectral properties of Almost Equitable Partitions (AEPs) to predict cluster stability.7 This domain requires a demonstrated ability to handle bifurcations, mean-field limits, and the interplay between structural symmetry and functional outcome.9

### **Nonequilibrium Thermodynamics and Critical Phenomena**

A "spectral self" system is fundamentally an open system operating far from equilibrium. Necessary expertise includes the landscape-flux theory, entropy production, and the study of phase transitions on graphs.11 PIs must understand how energy dissipation drives self-organization and how "curl flux" provides the dynamical origin for oscillations in neural and biological systems.11

### **Functional Analysis and Mathematical Physics**

Rigorous modeling of transport and heat equations on networks requires expertise in operator semigroups, self-adjoint operators, and heat kernel bounds.13 This ensures that the proposed research is grounded in the formal mathematics of infinite and locally finite graphs, addressing essential self-adjointness, Markov uniqueness, and stochastic completeness.14

## **Technical Profiles: Top PI Candidates**

The following section provides a detailed technical analysis of the top-ranked PIs, justifying their "Prime" status and identifying the specific intersections of their work that align with spectral-self-adjacent research.

### **1\. Jin Wang (Stony Brook University)**

Jin Wang stands as the premier candidate for leading programs that require a deep synthesis of nonequilibrium statistical mechanics and network stability. His research program, particularly the "Nonequilibrium Landscape and Flux Theory," provides a global quantitative understanding of systems operating far from equilibrium.11 Wang’s framework posits that the driving force behind a network's dynamics is determined by two components: the gradient of a potential landscape, which ensures local stability and attraction to functional states, and a curl probability flux, which represents the degree of detailed balance breaking and drives coherent oscillations.11

Wang has demonstrated the application of this theory across remarkably diverse systems, from the global stability of neural networks responsible for cognitive processes to the differentiation and reprogramming of single cells. His 2024 publications in *PNAS* and *Advanced Science* underscore a prolific and contemporary research output. Specifically, his work on quantifying the landscape-flux via single-cell transcriptomics to uncover cell cycle mechanisms demonstrates a high level of computational and experimental integration. For a "spectral self" program, Wang provides the most credible physical origin for how a system can maintain an autonomous identity through the quantification of energy dissipation and entropy production.11

### **2\. Adilson E. Motter (Northwestern University)**

Adilson Motter is a central figure in the dynamical behavior of complex systems, specializing in synchronization and the control of network dynamics. As the Charles and Emma Morrison Professor of Physics and the Director of the Center for Network Dynamics at Northwestern, Motter possesses the institutional weight to lead large-scale research centers.9 His research into "converse symmetry breaking"—where a system’s asymmetry is required for the stability of a symmetric state—provides a profound structural logic for autonomous network coordination.9

Motter’s track record of applying nonlinear dynamics to critical infrastructure is exceptional. His group derived the conditions for the spontaneous synchronization of power grids, suggesting preventive care models that mitigate failures before they cascade.18 Furthermore, his work extends to biological memory, evidenced by recent research on how bacterial cells transmit memories to offspring through network states.17 Motter’s ability to coordinate multi-institution work is evidenced by his extensive co-authorship list and his leadership in the American Physical Society (APS).9 He is a viable prime for programs focusing on the self-healing and resilient properties of large-scale physical and biological networks.18

### **3\. Surya Ganguli (Stanford University)**

Surya Ganguli represents the ideal PI for programs at the intersection of theoretical physics and modern machine learning. His work is characterized by the use of nonequilibrium thermodynamics and statistical mechanics to understand the dynamics of learning in deep neural networks.20 Ganguli’s landmark paper on "Deep Unsupervised Learning using Nonequilibrium Thermodynamics" established a framework for using Markov chains to gradually destroy and restore structure in data, a precursor to the modern diffusion models that define current generative AI.21

Ganguli’s lab maintains a high volume of funding and a massive collaborative reach, with co-authors spanning Stanford, Google DeepMind, and the Alan Turing Institute.19 His recent work on "Holistic Evaluation of Language Models" and "Data Pruning" demonstrates his influence on the scaling laws of artificial intelligence.19 For research into spectral selfhood, Ganguli offers a pathway to implement autonomous structure-building in AI systems through the lens of transient chaos and exponential expressivity.20 His credibility with agencies like DARPA and the NSF makes him a powerful advocate for high-risk, high-reward theories.19

### **4\. Ginestra Bianconi (Queen Mary University of London)**

Ginestra Bianconi is the leading authority on the interplay between geometry, topology, and dynamics on complex networks. Her recent focus on higher-order networks—captured in her 2021 book *Higher-Order Networks: An Introduction to Simplicial Complexes*—addresses interactions that involve two or more nodes, triangles, or tetrahedra.6 Bianconi’s research highlights how topological signals defined on these higher-order structures drive phenomena such as topological synchronization.23

In 2025, she proposed a statistical mechanics action for gravity derived from the quantum relative entropy between the true metric of spacetime and the metric induced by matter fields.22 This indicates an ability to bridge complexity science with fundamental theoretical physics at the highest level. Bianconi serves as the Editor-in-Chief of *JPhys Complexity*, placing her at the center of the scholarly community.6 Her work is essential for programs that treat the "self" as a topological emergent property of a system’s metric and dynamical degrees of freedom.24

### **5\. Massimiliano Esposito (University of Luxembourg)**

Massimiliano Esposito is a theoretical physicist specializing in the thermodynamics of open chemical reaction networks (CRNs). His research aims to advance nonequilibrium thermodynamics as a unifying framework for understanding energy and information flows across physical, chemical, and biological systems.3 As the head of the Complex Systems and Statistical Mechanics group, Esposito manages a significant research unit funded by the European Research Council (ERC) and the Luxembourg National Research Fund.3

Esposito’s work is particularly relevant for the study of energy transduction and active phase separation.27 He has formulated the nonequilibrium thermodynamics for CRNs by drawing "wisdom from stochastic thermodynamics," allowing for the characterization of the energetics of phenomena like synchronization in chemical systems.26 His teaching and outreach on the conceptual clarity of thermodynamics make him an excellent lead for programs requiring the communication of deep math to cross-disciplinary audiences.3

### **6\. Dimitrios Giannakis (Dartmouth College)**

Dimitrios Giannakis is a pioneer in data-driven, operator-theoretic methods for dynamical systems. His work centers on the use of Koopman and transfer operators to represent nonlinear dynamics in state space through linear actions on observables.30 This framework enables the use of linear operator theory for the supervised and unsupervised learning of complex systems like climate dynamics.29

Giannakis’s recent "Spectral Exterior Calculus" (SEC) framework represents vector fields as linear combinations of frame elements constructed from Laplacian eigenfunctions.63 This approach ensures that reconstructed dynamical flows are compatible with the nonlinear geometry of the manifold.63 Holding a Vannevar Bush Faculty Fellowship—one of the DOD's most prestigious single-investigator awards—Giannakis has the proven ability to lead high-level research programs with substantial funding.28 He is the ideal PI for programs focusing on the extraction of coherent spectral features from noisy, high-dimensional experimental data.29

### **7\. Matthias Keller (University of Potsdam)**

Matthias Keller is a Professor of Graph Theory whose research operates at the heart of spectral geometry and Dirichlet forms. His work is characterized by extreme mathematical rigor, particularly regarding unbounded operators on graphs and Schrödinger operators.14 He is a key researcher in the Collaborative Research Centre (SFB 1294\) "Data Assimilation," which integrates data and models through spectral methods.14

Keller’s 668-page book *Graphs and Discrete Dirichlet Spaces* (2021) provides a complete account of infinite graphs, addressing self-adjointness and spectral estimates.14 For a program requiring formal mathematical proofs for the well-posedness of heat flows on complex geometries, Keller is the primary authority. His collaborations with international teams on quasilinear operators and Agmon estimates demonstrate his capability to host funded subcontracts for specialized theory development.14

## **Strategic Synthesis and Partnership Readiness**

The transition from identifying a target list to establishing a functional research partnership requires an understanding of the PI’s institutional constraints and the value of the subcontractor's specialized implementation. For a program focusing on "spectral self" concepts, the partnership should be framed around the most rigorous adjacent problems—such as the thermodynamic limits of network control or the spectral properties of autonomous synchronization.9

### **Recommendations for Immediate Outreach**

1. **Prioritize Interdisciplinary Centers:** Focus on PIs affiliated with institutes like NICO (Northwestern), the Alan Turing Institute (UK), or the Complex Systems group at Luxembourg. these centers are designed to host collaborative research and simplify subcontracting mechanics.3  
2. **Target High-Gravity Fellowships:** Researchers holding DOD Vannevar Bush Fellowships (Giannakis) or ERC Advanced Grants (Uysal, Bianconi) possess the maximum flexibility in their funding and are often seeking specialized partners to deliver the prototype or system-design layer of their high-level theories.22  
3. **Leverage "Integrative Think Tanks":** Programs like SAMBa at the University of Bath provide a formal mechanism (the ITT workshops) for external partners to interact with PIs on specific problem formulations.34

By aligning specialized implementation capabilities with the foundational theories of nonequilibrium landscape-flux and higher-order topological synchronization, a subcontractor can provide the necessary experimental "teeth" to a PI's theoretical framework, ensuring a competitive and credible position in the landscape of large-scale research grants.11
